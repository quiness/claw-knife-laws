California Knife Laws

Last verified January 15, 2025 · Updated for 2025

California allows open carry of most knives but restricts concealed carry of dirks and daggers. Switchblades and automatic knives with blades 2 inches or longer are illegal. There is no statewide blade length limit, but local cities like Los Angeles and San Francisco may impose stricter rules. No statewide preemption exists, so always check local ordinances.

Open Carry Laws in California

California has no general prohibition on openly carrying knives. Fixed-blade knives must be carried in a sheath worn openly suspended from the waist to avoid being classified as concealed. Folding knives may be carried openly without restriction.

  • Cal. Penal Code § 20200—Openly worn sheath knife not concealed
Concealed Carry Laws in California

Concealed carry depends on the type of knife. Folding knives (non-locking and locking) may generally be carried concealed. Dirks, daggers, and fixed-blade knives cannot be concealed. A “dirk or dagger” is broadly defined as any knife or instrument capable of ready use as a stabbing weapon that may inflict great bodily injury or death.

  • Cal. Penal Code § 21310—Carrying concealed dirk or dagger
  • Cal. Penal Code § 16470—Definition of dirk or dagger
Blade Length Limits

No statewide blade length limit. California does not impose a statewide blade length limit. However, local ordinances may restrict blade length (e.g., Los Angeles restricts concealed carry of knives with blades over 3 inches).

Switchblade & Automatic Knife Laws

Switchblade Illegal

Switchblades with a blade of 2 inches or longer are illegal to carry on the person, possess in the passenger or driver area of a vehicle in a public place, sell, or transfer. Switchblades with a blade shorter than 2 inches are not prohibited under this section.

  • Cal. Penal Code § 21510—Switchblade knife prohibitions
  • Cal. Penal Code § 17235—Definition of switchblade knife

Automatic Knife Illegal

Automatic knives fall under the switchblade definition in California. If the blade is 2 inches or longer and opens automatically, it is illegal.

Assisted Opening Legal

Assisted-opening knives are not prohibited under California law. The switchblade definition (§ 17235) explicitly excludes knives that open with thumb pressure on the blade or a thumb stud, provided the knife has a detent or mechanism that resists opening or biases the blade toward its closed position.

  • Cal. Penal Code § 17235—Exclusion for assisted-opening knives

Ballistic Knife Illegal

Ballistic knives are prohibited under both federal law and California Penal Code.

  • Cal. Penal Code § 21110—Ballistic knife prohibitions
Restricted Locations

Schools, government buildings, courts, airports, and other locations as defined by statute.

It is illegal to bring any knife with a blade longer than 2.5 inches onto K-12 school grounds (§ 626.10). Knives with blades over 4 inches are prohibited in state or local public buildings and at required-open public meetings (§ 171b).

  • Cal. Penal Code § 626.10—Weapons on school grounds
  • Cal. Penal Code § 171b—Weapons in state or local public buildings
Preemption & Local Laws

California does not have statewide preemption for knife laws. Local municipalities (e.g., Los Angeles, San Francisco) may enact stricter knife ordinances than state law. Always check local regulations before carrying.

California Knife Law FAQ

Can you open carry a knife in California?

Yes. California has no general prohibition on openly carrying knives. Fixed-blade knives must be carried in a sheath worn openly suspended from the waist (Cal. Penal Code § 20200). Folding knives may be carried openly without restriction.

Can you concealed carry a knife in California?

It depends on the type of knife. Folding knives (non-locking and locking) may generally be carried concealed. Dirks, daggers, and fixed-blade knives cannot be concealed (Cal. Penal Code § 21310). A dirk or dagger is broadly defined as any knife capable of ready use as a stabbing weapon.

Are switchblades legal in California?

No. Switchblades with a blade of 2 inches or longer are illegal to carry, possess in a vehicle, sell, or transfer (Cal. Penal Code § 21510). Switchblades with blades shorter than 2 inches are not prohibited.

Are automatic knives legal in California?

No. Automatic knives fall under the switchblade definition. If the blade is 2 inches or longer and opens automatically, it is illegal (Cal. Penal Code § 21510).

Is there a blade length limit in California?

There is no statewide blade length limit. However, schools restrict knives with blades over 2.5 inches (§ 626.10), and government buildings restrict blades over 4 inches (§ 171b). Local ordinances may also apply.

Are assisted-opening knives legal in California?

Yes. Assisted-opening knives are explicitly excluded from the switchblade definition under Cal. Penal Code § 17235, provided the knife has a detent or mechanism that resists opening or biases the blade toward its closed position.
